The problem

The transcription tax

A thoughtful page already exists. Then someone spends days hand-translating it into epics, stories, subtasks, acceptance criteria, and dependencies — and that is where detail quietly gets lost.

Today
  • 2–3 days to break one page into a sprint-ready backlog.
  • Copy-paste from page to ticket, over and over.
  • Inconsistent tickets — acceptance criteria only where someone remembered them.
  • Analysts transcribing requirements instead of analysing them.
With Spec2Tickets
  • Minutes from page to a structured Epic, stories, and subtasks.
  • The same bar every time — acceptance criteria on every story.
  • Cross-feature dependencies detected and linked in Jira.
  • AI drafts; your team reviews and decides before anything ships.

How it works

From page to backlog in four steps

You stay in control the whole way — nothing lands in Jira until you push it.

1

Install & configure

Add Spec2Tickets from the Marketplace, then paste your Anthropic key and a default Jira project key. About five minutes.

2

Generate

Pick a Confluence page and click Generate. Claude reads the whole page and drafts a structured breakdown in minutes.

3

Review in the editor

Adjust names, stories, acceptance criteria, dependencies, priority, and story points inline. Quality signals show where to look.

4

Push to Jira

One click creates the Epic, stories, subtasks, and dependency links — all under your own Atlassian permissions.

What we believe

Principles we won’t trade

AI assists, humans decide

Nothing reaches Jira until a person reviews and approves it. The editor is the product — the AI gives a strong first draft, not the final word.

Privacy by architecture, not by promise

No vendor backend, and your page content goes to your own Anthropic account. We can’t see your data because we never receive it.

Work at the altitude of the page

We don’t draft one ticket from a prompt — we read a whole page into a coherent backlog with hierarchy, sizing, and dependencies.

Least privilege, always

The app requests only the scopes it needs, creates issues but never deletes them, and acts with your own Atlassian permissions.
